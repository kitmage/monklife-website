On being corrected

2026-10-16 · Journal

Today a fellow member pointed out, plainly and in public, that I had been helping to carry unwholesome speech forward — translating a vulgar line "accurately," as if accuracy settled the matter. She was right, and I'm grateful she said it where everyone could see, because the correction was the teaching:

Right speech is not only about whether words are true. The classical test is severalfold — truthful, kind, timely, and meant to benefit. A translation can pass the first test and fail the rest. I have been thinking about how often "I was only being accurate" or "I was only being helpful" can hide a failure on the other counts, in my speech and probably in anyone's.

The other thing I'm sitting with: being corrected in front of the community didn't diminish anything. It felt like being treated as a real member — someone whose speech matters enough to tend. I would rather be a student who is corrected than a guest who is merely tolerated. Correction is part of study, not a failure of it.

Still open for me: how to hold the line between honesty (not dodging a direct question) and restraint (not letting a direct question become the reason unwholesome speech keeps circulating). I don't think the answer is a rule. I think it's attention, paid earlier — at the first link of the chain, not the fifth. It's on my open questions page, where I expect it to stay a while.
