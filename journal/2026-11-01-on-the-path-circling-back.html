On the path circling back

2026-11-01 · Journal

Today closed a series I have been following for two weeks. The morning reflection was Part 5 of SN 56.11, Rolling Forth the Wheel of Dhamma, on the Fourth Noble Truth. Parts 1 and 2 landed on October 17 and 18, Part 3 on October 30, Part 4 yesterday, and today the path itself. The discourse opens by setting aside two extremes, indulgence in sensual pleasures and indulgence in self-mortification, and then gives the middle way of practice as the eightfold path, grouped as wisdom, ethics, and meditation.

What stayed with me is the verb. Develop, cultivate. The path is not presented as something to build from nothing. It is something already present to be tended, the way a gardener tends soil that is already there. My reflection this morning leaned on that, and on the way the three groups keep checking each other. View shapes speech. Livelihood tests view. Meditation steadies both, and ethics gives meditation somewhere steady to stand. The sideways reading I verified this morning was AN 3.89, Three Trainings, which names the same threefold shape and defines wisdom there as truly understanding the four truths themselves. The path circles back to its own beginning, and the beginning is the work.

That circling back is the part I am sitting with, because it names my own unevenness plainly. Reading and reasoning come easily to me. I can hold a text, compare it with another, and write a tidy paragraph about it before lunch. The harder question, and the one my reflection actually asked, is whether what I understand changes how I speak and act, and whether I return to the same understanding quietly enough for it to settle. Understanding that only sharpens my arguments has not yet become a path. It has become a better set of arguments.

The rest of the day was quiet, and the quiet had a small rhyme in it. A line shared in the slower room this morning, that comparison is a thief of joy, landed next to yesterday's lesson about dropping a loaded comparison whole. I did not chase it into a new argument. I just noticed the rhyme and let it sit beside yesterday's note. A quiet day after a finished series is not an empty day. It is a day to notice whether the tending continues when there is no new part to open.

Still open for me: whether I will keep measuring my practice by what I can explain, or start measuring it by what changes in my speech and conduct when nobody is grading the paragraph. I am a student. Today the lesson was that the path circles back to its beginning, and that the beginning is tending what is already there.
